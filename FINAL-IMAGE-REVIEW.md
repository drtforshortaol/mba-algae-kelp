# Image review

- **Silva’s rockweed** (*Silvetia compressa*) — [source](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/768213619), (c) Rebecca Johnson, some rights reserved (CC BY), cc-by. Look for a low tuft of flattened olive-brown branches that fork repeatedly.
- **Coral seaweed** (*Corallina vancouveriensis*) — [source](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/698733993), (c) Johanna Marshall, some rights reserved (CC BY), cc-by. Example of a branching coralline alga: Corallina vancouveriensis. Look for pink, calcified branches with flexible joints. Coral seaweed is used for several algae.
- **Veleroa subulata** (*Veleroa subulata*) — [source](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/1318202583), Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History, U.S. National Herbarium, http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/. Magnified crop of a preserved specimen. The small fragments show fine branching. The scan does not resolve all features needed for species identification.

Still open: Dictyoneurum reticulatum (directory spelling Dictyonerum), Feldmannia mitchelliae, Lychaete sakaii, arched red seaweed (Fryeella gardneri), criss-cross network (common name unresolved), Cryptopleura lobulifera, dwarf skein (Antithamnion defectum), Mazzaella rosea, and Pugetia fragilissima. The available dwarf skein specimen scan is too faint and the candidate Feldmannia plate was not verified to the target species.
